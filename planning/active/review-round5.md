## Checked and sound (no finding)

- **Coverage argument.** GEOS TopologyPreservingSimplifier keeps the Douglas-Peucker bound. Every removed vertex lies within tol of the chord that replaces it, and the region between the old polyline and the chord lies inside the convex stadium around the chord. So data ⊂ simplify(data, t/2) ⊕ t/2. It never drops a ring or a component, so small islands and holes survive as rings of at least 4 coordinates. `buffer(t)` then leaves at least t/2 of margin, and the closing `simplify(t/2)` moves the boundary by at most t/2. The worst-case margin is 0, the same as the old `buffer(t).simplify(t)`, so this is no regression. GEOS's internal buffer-input simplifier (1% of the distance) only removes concavities, so it only enlarges the buffer.
- **Mitre limit 2.0.** I probed spikes at 1–170° with t = 1, 10 and 50. The mitre buffer contained the round buffer every time: the bevel sits at 2t from the vertex, beyond the radius-t disk.
